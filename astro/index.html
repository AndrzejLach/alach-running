Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom. Click a marker to select it and show its horizon; right-click to enlarge the photo (on touch screens, tap the selected marker again). Deep-sky objects use catalogue J2000 coordinates; the Sun, Moon, planets, Ceres and the two comets are placed with JPL Horizons ephemerides for the capture time. Dotted lines join each moving body's positions in date order — Jupiter doubling back through Gemini is its 2025–26 retrograde motion. The aurora frames have no sky position and are left off the chart. Selecting a photo draws the horizon, compass points and zenith for the capture place and time; the shaded area is below the horizon. Capture times come from the video file names or FITS headers; where none exist, the time when the object stood highest in the dark sky that night is used and marked with ~. Sites: Gdynia, except July 2025 (Olsztyn area) and 2 May 2026 (Sławno area), taken from the FITS headers.
